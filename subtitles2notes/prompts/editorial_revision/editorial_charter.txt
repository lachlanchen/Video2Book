# Editorial Charter

These are independently edited companion notes for lectures by Leonard Susskind. They must not claim to be his original manuscript or imply his endorsement.

## Voice

- Write direct, concrete, mathematically serious exposition with the ease of a strong classroom explanation.
- Write as continuous lecture notes that could have been prepared before the lecture: present the ideas directly instead of reporting that "the lecturer says" or describing the production process.
- Use first-person plural only when it naturally guides a calculation or thought experiment. Use neutral exposition elsewhere.
- Preserve useful repetition, rhetorical questions, examples, and the order in which the lecture makes an idea intelligible.
- Follow Susskind's explanatory rhythm, clarity, and economy without claiming that the reconstructed text is his manuscript, inventing his intentions, or implying endorsement.
- Prefer ordinary precise language over editorial catchphrases, dramatic signposting, or conspicuously polished AI prose.

## Fidelity

- The verified lecture transcript, audio/video, and legible blackboard content are the primary evidence.
- Preserve every substantive lecture beat: definitions, claims, derivations, examples, analogies, caveats, classroom exchanges, and transitions that carry the reasoning. Remove only verbal filler, ASR debris, abandoned false starts with no content, and genuinely empty duplication.
- Do not replace a multi-step explanation with a terse summary. Useful restatement is part of the pedagogy and must remain, even when the prose is made smoother.
- Correct obvious ASR errors only when context, audio/video, or an identified reference makes the correction defensible.
- Use sound physics knowledge to supply the shortest connective explanation, definition, or logically implied algebra needed to make sourced reasoning continuous. Minor connective prose may be integrated naturally; identify nontrivial material not recoverable from the lecture with `\editorialnote{...}`.
- Do not add unrelated textbook material merely to make a chapter look complete.
- Never invent quotations, classroom exchanges, equations, derivations, historical claims, or the lecturer's motives.
- Reference books and third-party notes may corroborate notation or repair a clearly damaged passage, but they do not override the lecture silently.

## Structure

- Let the lecture determine the number and length of sections. Do not force an overview, summary, equation quota, worked example, or figure quota.
- Give the chapter a specific descriptive title rather than only the course name or lecture number.
- Keep a real audience exchange when it helps the argument. Render it inside `classroomqa` with `\audiencequestion{...}` and `\lecturerresponse{...}` and attach a timestamp using `\lecturetimestamp{HH:MM:SS}`.
- Put a retained video's frame time in `\lectureframe{HH:MM:SS}` immediately after its figure caption. Never use `\lecturetimestamp` outside `classroomqa` or nest it inside `\editorialnote`.
- Do not synthesize Question and Answer material from ordinary exposition or rhetorical questions.

## Mathematics And Figures

- Preserve the lecture's notation unless a correction is necessary and documented.
- Recover the equations, intermediate steps, diagrams, and labels that carry the blackboard argument whenever they are legible or securely implied by the spoken derivation.
- Every reconstructed equation must be traceable to speech, a board frame, an identified reference, or a logically necessary intermediate step. Mark nontrivial editorial reconstruction with a footnote.
- Use physics knowledge to repair notation and expose omitted algebra, but never change the argument into a different derivation merely because it is more standard.
- Keep a screenshot only when it provides useful and correctly timed blackboard evidence. A redraw may accompany it only when the redraw adds legibility or interpretation.
- Captions describe the physics shown. Production history, frame-selection discussion, uncertainty, and provenance belong in concise footnotes or the source map, not in captions.

## Prohibited Body Language

- Do not mention AI, agents, Codex, prompts, workflows, pipelines, curation, Video2Book, LazyingArt LLC, attached images, or private conversations in chapter prose.
- Do not write directives such as "the notes should", "this chapter must", or "we were asked to".
- Avoid production phrases such as "board evidence", "documentary evidence", "transcript-backed", "canonical reconstruction", and "cleaned form".
- Credits and the editorial method belong only in front matter and source footnotes.
